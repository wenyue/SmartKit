# Authoring Decisions

## Keep ordinary review decisions together

The entry follows the human's work: establish context, inspect and judge, explain complete file groups, then resolve the choice and resume. Standards, required disclosure, M/O identity, grouping, progress, action meanings, priorities, and pending intent stay together. Common link generation remains in that entry; PR-specific identity and display context belong with preparation.

## Give report presentation one owner

[Report Format](references/report-format.md) owns the complete template, presentation rules, explanation depth, and send-time format checks. Keeping those details together makes the entry's review decisions easier to follow without mixing presentation into preparation or delivery. The reference is mandatory before every report kind, including ordinary review; this adds a required reading step rather than reducing the total material needed for an ordinary review. A missing format pauses its dependent report. Inspection and complete finding disclosure remain in the entry so concise presentation cannot reduce review rigor.

## Load conditional knowledge at its boundary

Preparation owns PR/remote evidence, Viewed filtering, and workspace choices. Delivery owns full submitted/outgoing coverage, readiness, writes, readbacks, and reconciliation of actual effects. The entry decides what the user requested and whether to advance; delivery reports what is ready or actually happened. This boundary prevents an interrupted push or supplementary finding from erasing a pending Pass without duplicating the conversation policy.

The helper's CLI help owns mechanical capabilities, request fields, state, outputs, and retry instructions beside the executable interface. No helper reference repeats that interface. Delivery still determines semantic coverage and authorized effects, including when the helper cannot represent the complete plan and a manual path is needed. Keeping interface prose in help does not change execution or make the helper a semantic reviewer.

## Separate inspection coverage from report granularity

Actual non-test changes and necessary context determine review coverage. Two leading judgments give the human the assessment before implementation comparisons; they hold complete item-keyed findings. Report Format owns the fixed skeleton, full-path title paragraphs, and every file boundary, while quoted comparisons show each implementation at the depth its difficulty requires. Concise reporting and grouping cannot hide a local finding or average it away. Examples illustrate that contract rather than define another one.

## Derive choices from facts and retained intent

Stable action meanings and a small ordered decision table keep recommendations predictable without cataloging every conversation. Preparation's workspace priority serves a different decision from review remedy priority. Facts and external blockers are not menus. Omitting routine Stop options keeps choices focused without removing explicit user control or safety stops; a recommendation never becomes authority.

Full-workspace delivery and the main reading sequence have different scopes. Test files remain in commits and checks but outside semantic review; unreviewed tests do not gain Viewed eligibility from checks. Pass composes delivery with advancement, while only-deliver has the same effects and stays in place. Supplementary findings suspend that intent until resolved or explicitly retained; they neither cancel it nor acquire automatic acceptance.

## Let examples teach distinct transitions

One compact complete report illustrates Report Format. Independent conversation examples then use short scenarios and explicitly abbreviated excerpts to show meaningful choices, effects, and next positions without repeating whole reports. Their premises preserve when an action is available. They do not weaken complete supplementary or repair reporting, own policy, or establish general model reliability.
