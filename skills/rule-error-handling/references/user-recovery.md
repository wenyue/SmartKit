# User Recovery

## Leave a practical way to proceed

Preserve input, drafts, completed work, and still-valid visible state as the contract requires. A failed refresh need not erase prior content; a rejected save need not close the editor. Make material stale or unsaved status clear and restore usable interaction through existing UI state and lifecycle when a supported action remains.

Offer the simplest supported action that helps: waiting, retrying a safe unfinished step, signing in, choosing a valid location, or contacting a responsible operator. Include technical details such as an affected path when useful to that audience.

## Assign human repair to someone who can perform it

Rely on human repair only when an actor in the actual deployment has the access, authority, tools, and ability to perform it with reasonable effort.

If direct repair is unavailable, use a feasible supported escalation route when one exists. If no capable, authorized actor can perform required remediation, resolve ownership and disposition before implementing it. A product may ask users for bounded choices under an established contract; unresolved recovery or data-loss policy needs its owner's decision first.

A human handoff must transfer impact and audience-safe evidence and assign completion and feedback. Displaying contact instructions alone does not complete it.

## Make remedial completion observable

Make remedial success, failure, and material interruption visible to those who need to act. On failure, re-enable controls or retain an actionable state and preserve the original incident and new failure through their owners. If feedback delivery fails, use an established feasible fallback without recursive notifications or replacing the operation's outcome.

Keep feedback available while action is still needed. A transient notification is insufficient when unresolved conditions would otherwise disappear from view.
